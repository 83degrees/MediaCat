# DDR-03-001: Exclude Legacy Top-Level Providers from Schema v3 and Preserve the Schema-v2 Rollback Package

## Identifier provenance

Previous identifier: `DDR-001` under the pre-federated governance model.

## Decision

The legacy schema-v2 top-level `providers` mapping was not migrated into the
schema-v3 stored item or normalized returned record. Schema v3 instead retains
only the distinct execution-source `source.provider` field where applicable.

During the schema-v3 transition, the complete schema-v2 rollback package was
preserved: the byte-for-byte schema-v2 catalogue, its compatible Curated Media
implementation, the relevant activation configuration and the working legacy
lookup path. An unexpected dependency on the legacy top-level `providers`
mapping was to be handled by restoring that complete package, not by adding a
hybrid compatibility field to schema v3.

This DDR recovers the rationale for the decision already accepted through
`ASTV-32`; it does not make or revise the schema, provider or rollback decision.

## Context

The read-only snapshot reviewed under `ASTV-32` contained
`providers.radio_music.service` on all 11 schema-v2 catalogue items. No consumer
of the top-level `providers` mapping was found in the captured Curated Media
Python, ASTV or AdvMedia YAML, or other relevant captured configuration.

That evidence had material limits. The snapshot had no capture manifest,
runtime traces or proof covering uncaptured external consumers. The schema-v2
loader also preserved the mapping, and `curated_media.resolve_item` returned it
as part of the complete raw record. The review therefore established that no
known consumer had been found, but it could not prove that no external consumer
existed.

The legacy top-level mapping and the schema-v3 execution-source field have
different meanings. Legacy `providers` contained `radio_music` service
identifiers. `execution_methods.<execution_method>.source.provider` identifies
the technical system supplying or resolving one execution source. The legacy
mapping had no evidenced target-schema role and could not be reinterpreted as
`source.provider` without changing its meaning.

Governance Stabilisation finding `F-003` identified that this rationale was not
retained in the approved MediaCat architecture or lookup contract even though
the resulting design was already current.

## Options Considered

1. Exclude the legacy top-level mapping from schema v3 and preserve a complete,
   restorable schema-v2 rollback package. This was the selected option.
2. Carry the legacy mapping into schema v3 temporarily as an opaque hybrid
   compatibility field.
3. Reinterpret or migrate the legacy values into execution-source
   `source.provider`.
4. Remove the legacy mapping and schema-v2 materials without preserving a
   complete restorable lookup path.

## Decision Rationale

The selected option kept schema v3 aligned with its approved model. No known
consumer or target-schema purpose justified carrying the legacy top-level
mapping forward, and its semantics did not match `source.provider`.

The incomplete consumer evidence nevertheless made irreversible removal unsafe.
Preserving the complete schema-v2 package retained a reliable response to an
unexpected dependency without contaminating schema v3. The exact catalogue
alone was insufficient for rollback: recovery also required the compatible
implementation, activation configuration and lookup path needed to return the
original record.

A hybrid compatibility field was rejected because it would have introduced an
unapproved mixed schema, blurred the boundary between legacy and current
provider concepts, and hidden rather than exposed any unexpected dependency.
Restoring the coherent schema-v2 package made the compatibility boundary
explicit and testable.

## Consequences

- Schema v3 has no legacy top-level `providers` mapping.
- Legitimate execution-source `source.provider` values remain part of their
  applicable source structures and retain their existing meaning.
- During the transition, an unexpected legacy dependency required restoration
  of the complete schema-v2 package rather than a schema-v3 compatibility patch.
- After explicit closure of the rollback window, the schema-v2 package remains
  read-only historical evidence rather than an operationally maintained
  rollback option.
- The inability to prove the absence of every external consumer remains an
  explicit evidence limitation; it is not converted into a claim of absence.
- This rationale record does not change the current lookup contract,
  implementation or production state.
